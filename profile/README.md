## AFT Arrow Highlights

- **Density Changes Along The Run:** AFT Arrow compressible flow accounts for gas that expands or a steam line that condenses toward equipment you already specified.
- **Networks Of Gas, Not A Single Orifice:** AFT Arrow gas network solves branches, valves, and compressors on a system you operate.
- **Steam As A Process Fluid:** AFT Arrow steam piping is for headers and laterals you engineer, with properties that are not a water friction factor.
- **Choke And Sonic Limits Visible:** AFT Arrow process gas should show when a valve or a relief path you modeled is sonic, which a liquid solver will not.
- **Sister To Fathom, Different Physics:** Keep liquids in Fathom. Arrow is the compressible seat in that family.

## What Is AFT Arrow

AFT Arrow is Applied Flow Technology’s compressible pipe-flow analyzer. Plants open it for natural gas, air, nitrogen, steam, and similar duties on P&IDs they already own. AFT Arrow compressible flow is the physics. AFT Arrow gas network is the schematic of branches and machines. AFT Arrow steam piping is the steam-header case. AFT Arrow process gas is the overall job: supply to burners, instrument air, a flare lateral you were hired to check.

Fathom will happily accept a pipe that should have been steam and give a polite, wrong number. Arrow exists so that does not happen. Compressors, control valves, and heat transfer on gas have to be the equipment on your datasheet, not a liquid pump curve pasted into the wrong product.

Model only compressible systems you design, operate, or were contracted to analyze. Keep fluid packages with the revision. Do not size a pipeline you do not have survey data for.

## Starting with AFT Arrow

Prerequisites: A licensed seat you administer, a P&ID of gas or steam you are allowed to model, and composition or steam tables for that duty.

1. **Prepare:** Confirm the fluid is compressible. If it is a liquid loop, stop and use Fathom.
2. **Configure:** Build AFT Arrow gas network or AFT Arrow steam piping with plant tags and the right fluid package.
3. **Test:** Solve a known supply pressure and compare with a transmitter you trust.
4. **Refine:** Add valves and a compressor map, then watch AFT Arrow compressible flow for sonic warnings.
5. **Apply:** Issue AFT Arrow process gas cases the spec asked for, named from the operating modes.
6. **Maintain:** Archive fluid packages with the revision, and do not silently swap composition.

## Best Uses

- **Instrument Air You Engineer:** AFT Arrow gas network on a header you operate.
- **Steam Laterals To Turbines Or Tracers:** AFT Arrow steam piping with real properties.
- **Fuel Gas To Burners You Specified:** AFT Arrow process gas against the datasheet.
- **Relief Path You Were Hired To Check:** Watch choke in AFT Arrow compressible flow.
- **Do Not Model Cooling Water Here:** That is Fathom.
- **Do Not Model A City Main Here:** Municipal water is another family.
- **Composition From The Lab You Use:** Not a default air if the stream is not air.
- **One Header Family Per File When They Do Not Mix:** Steam and nitrogen stay honest.
- **Scenario Names From The Mode:** Startup, winter, turbine trip.
- **Relief Path As Its Own Case:** AFT Arrow compressible flow with the valve position the spec named.
- **Do Not Mix Steam And Nitrogen In One Header File:** AFT Arrow steam piping and AFT Arrow gas network stay honest apart.
